# [[899,173,3]] — contracted affine checkerboard core, exact d=3

## Direction & hypothesis

Targeting the `local-2d-single × weight-4` geometric frontier, whose record was
`[[923,173,3]]` (`g = 9k/n = 1.6869`). The affine mod-5 checkerboard grammar
credited in `notes/961-169-3.md` tops out at `g = 1.5827` in its raw
rectangle-completion form, so the opening was not in the base — the base
census over all rectangles `26 ≤ W, H ≤ 39` at `n ≤ 1010` gives 5,414 complete
cases and a best `g = 1.5827` (31×31, phases (3,1)), well below the record.

The opening was in **contraction**. Every existing record in this cell is a
rectangle completion that was then shrunk by qubit removal, and those recipes
(`research/geometric_grafts/945-172-3/`, `research/geometric_grafts/931-169-3/`)
were run as fixed recordings. What was not yet swept was a generalization of
their move set: they remove a qubit using one own-side stabilizer as a pivot of
degree 1 or 2; this campaign allows degree ≤ 3 and, more importantly, rejects a
move unless three things hold *simultaneously* — `k` unchanged, exact `d ≥ 3`,
and one connected stabilizer block.

## What was searched

`research/geometric_grafts/campaign.py`. Six base rectangles/phase/order
combinations from the census, six RNG seeds each, boundary-first candidate
ordering (the recorded contractions' successful ones all sit on the rim).

- **Exact `d ≥ 3`, not sampled.** For a CSS code, `v = e_q` commutes iff
  `H_opp[:, q] = 0`, and `v = e_q + e_r` commutes iff `H_opp[:, q] =
  H_opp[:, r]`. So "no weight ≤ 2 logical" reduces to column-equality
  enumeration plus rowspace membership — a *decision*, with no trials and no
  statistical caveat. Cross-checked against the trusted `gf2.in_rowspace` on
  5,000 random weight-≤6 vectors (0 mismatches) and on 3,000 rowspace
  combinations (all reduce to 0). It returns `True` on `codes/961-169-3.json`,
  `codes/925-173-3.json`, `codes/923-173-3.json`, `codes/961-1-31.json` and
  `False` on `codes/4-2-2.json`, `codes/100-50-2.json`.
- **Membership in O(support), not O(rank).** In RREF no row carries a second
  pivot column, so the reduction of `v` is exactly `v XOR R[i]` for each
  `i` with `v[piv[i]] = 1`, read off the *original* `v`. For a weight-≤2 probe
  that is two row XORs instead of a rank-400 Python loop: `_admissible` went
  from 0.043 s to 0.015 s per try, which is what made a 73-move chain
  affordable.
- **Moves.** `_pivot_move`: XOR own-side row `r` into every other own-side row
  covering `q`, delete `r`, delete `q` from both matrices. Commutation is
  preserved because no own-side row carries `q` any more. Degree 1 is the
  recorded recipes' `graft_r1`; degree 2–3 is the addition.
- **Acceptance** is one pass over two RREFs (rank → `k`, membership bases, and
  the block decomposition are all the same reduction): `k` unchanged, exact
  `d ≥ 3`, single stabilizer block.

The winning chain: base 34×29, phases `(px,pz)=(3,1)`, completion order 3 →
`n = 986, k = 173`; projected onto its unique nonzero-k stabilizer factor
(dropping seven k=0 spurs) → `n = 972`; **73 accepted moves** → `n = 899`.
Seed 0, deterministic.

## Dead ends

- **The cell I aimed at is not the cell I landed in.** Contraction raises max
  check weight from 4 to **8** (pivot XORs grow rows: histogram X = 325 rows of
  weight 4, plus 6/7/8-weight tails; Z similar) and max check diameter from
  `√2` to `√20 ≈ 4.47 > 4.0`. So this is a `weight-8 × local-2d-bilayer`
  entry, **not** `weight-4 × local-2d-single`. Beating `[[923,173,3]]` on its
  own cell needs moves that keep `w ≤ 4` and diameter `≤ 4` as hard
  constraints. `research/geometric_grafts/constrained.py` does exactly that
  with degree-1 moves and it stalls almost immediately: best `n = 969` at
  `k = 173` (all six seeds), `n = 952` at `k = 169` — nowhere near 923. The
  degree-2/3 moves that reach 899 are precisely the ones that break the caps.
- **The obvious board-advancing move, the direct sum, is barred.** Tiling the
  Z4 core `r`-fold gives `[[4r,2r,2]]` at `w=4`, Pareto-unbeaten at every `r`
  tried (up to `[[1000,500,2]]`) — and the verifier rejects it twice over:
  `tanner_connected` (r components) and `stabilizer_group_connected` (a
  bridged direct sum still has r independent blocks; `verify/test_verifier.py`
  encodes exactly this evasion). A `[[400,200,2]]` attempt was staged and
  failed the gate on both checks.
- **Weight-4 base census has no headroom.** Best `g = 1.5827` at `n=961`;
  a `W=40` sweep gives 1.547. The asymptote `k/n → 1/5` has not kicked in at
  `n ≤ 1000`.
- **Three zero rows.** The 73rd move left 3 empty Z rows, which the verifier
  counts as isolated check vertices (`tanner_connected` = 4 components). They
  carry no rowspace, so dropping them is exact and yields 1 component; the
  submitted JSON has them removed.
