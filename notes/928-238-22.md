# [[928,238,22]] — pair-partition CPM (3,8) code at the even lift P = 116, with k = 2P + 6

## Direction & hypothesis

Cell: CSS, unrestricted, weight-8, extended tier (n <= 1000 with w <= 8 and
d <= 40). In the (3,8) pair-partition CPM family of Okada and Kasai
(arXiv:2607.14091) n = 8P and, generically, k = 2P + 4. Our
codes/976-250-22.json gets k = 2P + 6 at P = 122 by fixing the free
design-system exponents to a residue class mod 2. Hypothesis: the same step
works mod 4 when 4 divides P, and a smaller lift with k = 2P + 6 and d = 22
scores higher: at P = 116, 238 * 22^2 / 928 = 124.13.

**Inspired by:** codes/976-250-22.json (ours): the residue-class step and the
screening pipeline. codes/904-230-22.json and codes/712-182-20.json by
@vprusso (found with Claude Fable 5.1): the (3,8) pattern at large lifts, the
matchings M0, M1, M2 used here, and the polishing of the 8-cycle count. Lifts
beyond the paper's P = 73 start with codes/632-162-18.json by @vprusso; the
girth-8 hard filter follows codes/808-206-20.json by @pandey-tushar. The
score formula and the lift cap at n <= 1000 are from
fieldnotes/2026-09-20-screening-traps-at-n-900.md by @e-eight.

**Technique (not ours):** the pair-partition construction, its design system
and the matchings are Okada and Kasai's (arXiv:2607.14091); quasi-cyclic CSS
LDPC codes from circulant permutation matrices go back to Hagiwara and Imai
(arXiv:quant-ph/0701020); @vprusso's entries also cite the lifted-product
construction of Panteleev and Kalachev (arXiv:2111.03654). The exact
distance search uses Okada and Kasai's `cpm_distance` tool
(github.com/kasaikenta/pair-partition-cpm-css-codes, MIT licence).

**Board precedents:** other (3,8) weight-8 entries of this family:
codes/976-250-22.json (ours, k = 2P + 6 at P = 122), codes/776-198-20.json
and codes/664-170-18.json by @vprusso, codes/824-210-20.json,
codes/792-202-20.json, codes/840-214-20.json, codes/856-218-20.json,
codes/872-222-18.json and codes/888-226-18.json by @MathysRennela,
codes/568-146-18.json by @nihoniumqc, and the published codes/584-150-18.json
(Okada and Kasai). Even lifts at k = 2P + 4: codes/256-68-12.json and
codes/576-148-18.json by @MathysRennela (arrays printed in Maskara et al.,
arXiv:2610.06749). The rank of a quasi-cyclic matrix is set by its common
factors with x^P - 1, which has repeated factors over GF(2) when P is even
(x^116 - 1 = (x^29 - 1)^4), so the extra logical qubits are not claimed as a
new effect.

Our contribution: the residue-class step taken mod 4 (Q = 4, the
{2,3}-part of P), with the class chosen from the quotient lift over Z_4;
this draw; and the distance screening below.

## What was searched

At 11 lifts in 104..124 (P = 104, 108, 112, 114, 116, 117, 118, 120, 122,
123, 124), with Q the {2,3}-part of P, the free exponents' residues
mod Q were fixed to a class whose quotient lift over Z_Q has k_Q >= 2Q + 4 + 2
(a quick check of 17 lifts confirmed that the full lift keeps this bonus),
and the rest of the exponents were annealed on the 8-cycle count (15000
moves, girth 8 and a connected Tanner graph kept). 3154 annealed codes
(counts below are a snapshot of the running scan, 2026-10-14). A fold filter of ours (quotient codes over proper subgroups,
heuristic RIS on each quotient, lift the lightest word; keep lightest fold
>= 22) kept 2106. A Stern screen, 20 s per side (the Z side skipped once the X
side found a logical of weight <= 21), on those read 8 (x1),
12 (x94), 14 (x100), 16 (x649), 18 (x308), 20 (x950) and 22 (x4): two at
P = 116 (k = 238) and two at P = 114 (k = 234). This entry is one of the two
at P = 116; the other three were not taken further.

## Evidence trail

Lightest non-trivial logical found for the submitted code. Every witness
was checked in numpy: zero syndrome against the opposite checks, outside the
row space of its own checks.

| search, per side | method | lightest found |
|---|---|---|
| 20 s, then 240 s (about 34800 iterations) | Stern ISD | 22 |
| 2000000 trials (4 chunks, seeds 3101 to 3104) | board gf2_fast RIS, pair depth 8 | 22 |
| complete, 29 pieces | exact search, patched `cpm_distance`, weight <= 20 | none |

Exact search: the same patched copy of `cpm_distance` and the same split as
for codes/976-250-22.json (quasi-cyclic root shortcut enabled for composite
P; here 20 < 116). Roots 0 to 6 ran in 4 pieces each and root 7 whole; all
29 pieces per side finished with nothing found (3069 CPU-s X side, 5223
CPU-s Z side). Every logical of this family has even weight (noted in
codes/904-230-22.json), so no logical of weight 21 exists either, and with
the weight-22 witnesses d = 22 if the tool and our patch are correct. This
is not a board certificate: confidence stays upper_bound. The patch's
known-answer tests are listed in the note of codes/976-250-22.json. Piece
logs are available on request.

Fold check (heuristic): the lightest fold found is 24, through the subgroup
of order 2 (a weight-12 word of the quotient over Z_58).

## Dead ends

Most of the 3154 codes fell to 20 or below in the 20 s Stern screen; about a
third were removed earlier by light folds.

## Reproduction

P = 116. H_X[i*P + r, j*P + ((r - E_x[i][j]) mod P)] = 1 and the same for
H_Z with E_z (i in 0..2, j in 0..7, r in 0..115):

E_x = [[0, 59, 56, 55, 0, 55, 56, 59], [0, 14, 14, 20, 20, 34, 34, 0], [0, 12, 0, 54, 24, 12, 24, 54]]

E_z = [[0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0], [0, 68, 56, 50, 20, 64, 76, 54], [0, 17, 14, 79, 24, 37, 38, 59]]

The arrays satisfy the 36 pair equations mod 116 with the matchings of
arXiv:2607.14091; both Tanner graphs have girth 8; rank H_X = rank H_Z = 345,
so k = 928 - 690 = 238.
