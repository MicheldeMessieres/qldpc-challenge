# [[468,36,20]] canonical lifted-product code LP_36^(2x3), rebuilt from the paper's appendix matrices

## Direction & hypothesis

Issue #2863 (item L4) listed arXiv:2607.28605 as a paper whose abstract
claims codes absent from the board: [[468,36,20]] and [[952,112,17]]. The
main text attributes its instances to Cain et al. (arXiv:2603.28627), whose
Appendix A carries only the n > 1000 codes, but the appendix of
arXiv:2607.28605 itself prints the base matrices of both in-range instances.
The question was whether they rebuild to their stated parameters and whether
either is Pareto-advancing on the board's four-axis test. On the corrected
dominance test [[468,36,20]] was the strongest claim on the issue's list,
undominated at every check weight from 4 to 11.

## What was searched

The canonical lifted product LP_l(A, A*) over R = F_2[x]/(x^l + 1), with
H_X = B(A (x) I, I (x) B) and H_Z^T = B(I (x) B ; A (x) I), B = A* the
transpose with x replaced by x^-1 and B(.) the circulant binarization (the
paper's Eq. 5). For LP_36^(2x3): A = (A' | q_1) with
A' = [[1, 1 + x^11 + x^29], [1, 0]] and q_1 = [1; x^8 + x^13 + x^16 + x^17],
l = 36. Both binarized check matrices are 216 x 468 of full rank, so k = 36,
and the check weights run 7 to 10. The same builder gives
LP_28^(3x5) = [[952,112,17]] (check weight 9, reads 17), which is dominated by
18 board entries and is not filed.

## Evidence trail

Both witnesses were re-verified in GF(2) before they were written;
verify/qldpc_verify.py re-checks them.

| instrument | trials | seed | lightest logical |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| accelerated RIS, pair depth 32 (research/kit/surrogate.py) | 500,000 | 5 | 20 |
| accelerated RIS, pair depth 32 | 10,000,000 | 41 | 20 (X logical) |
| the gate's own refutation (verify/validate_candidate.py) | 8,000 | gate | nothing below 20 |

The map (j, t, s) -> (t, j, s) on the left block's 3 x 3 tiles and
(p, q, s) -> (q, p, s) on the right block's 2 x 2 tiles carries H_X onto H_Z,
so d_X = d_Z and the Z witness is the image of the X witness. Final claim:
d <= 20 on both sides, a witnessed upper bound at 10,000,000 trials per side;
the paper states d = 20 exact. The single-block annihilator attack was not
run: it is built for two-block GB codes and does not apply to an LP block.
The cell's leaders have been attacked at 300,000,000 GPU trials per side;
this entry has not yet.

## Dead ends

[[952,112,17]] rebuilds exactly and is dominated by 18 entries at check
weight <= 9 ([[872,222,18]], [[576,148,18]], [[888,226,18]], [[776,198,20]]
among them). The n > 1000 instances in Cain et al. are outside the board's
range and, as the issue thread records, could not be confirmed at a depth the
gate accepts.

`research/kit/surrogate.py` (fast backend) for the readings, and
`verify/validate_candidate.py` for the gate.

One disagreement is worth recording, because it nearly went the other way and
the resolution is the reason this entry rests on two builds. An independent
rebuild in this campaign read a weight-16 logical on both sides and was
reported as a distance-inflation catch against the paper's d = 20. That claim
was withdrawn: the builder in question had already produced two silent bugs
(a circulant that inflated every check weight by the ring order, and a
double-build that fed a tuple back in as a matrix), each caught only because
n, k and w disagreed with the paper -- which is exactly the class of bug that
can preserve all three and still produce a different code. H_Z admits more than
one arrangement of the same factors at n = 468, k = 36, w = 10, so those three
numbers do not pin it down. The d = 20 reading rests on the 10,000,000-trial
pass and on the automorphism, which reduces the claim to a single X witness
rather than two independent searches.

## Reproduction

Build `A` as above over `F_2[x]/(x^36 + 1)`, `B = A*`, and the two check
matrices by the binarized Kronecker products; index the left block by `(j, t)`
over the 3 x 3 tiles row-major with the ring position last, then the right
block by `(p, q)` over the 2 x 2 tiles the same way. The builder is committed
with the campaign record of issue #2863, at
`research/campaigns/openalex-harvest-2863/lp_canonical.py`, and reproduces
both check matrices of this entry exactly; run it and compare `X` and `Z`
against `codes/468-36-20.json`. The JSON carries both check matrices and both
witnesses, so the entry also verifies without the construction.
