# [[200,16,17]] routing code of Zhang et al., rebuilt from the paper's routing rule

## Direction & hypothesis

Issue #2863 (item L2) listed arXiv:2606.25330 as a paper whose abstract claims
codes absent from the board: [[200,24,14]] and [[200,16,17]] at check weight
11, plus a weight-7 family. The paper publishes no check matrices, but its
construction is a rule that determines every check, so the question was
whether the instances rebuild to their stated parameters and whether any of
them is Pareto-advancing on the board's four-axis test (n, k, d, check
weight, dominators allowed only in a locality class at or below the
candidate's). The appeal of the family, that every non-local coupling is
parallel to the others, is a connectivity property the board does not price;
it is recorded here and plays no part in the ranking.

## What was searched

The routing rule, implemented from Section II of the paper: on the torus
Z_l x Z_m the data qubits sit at sites (i, j) with i + j even, X syndromes at
i odd and j even, Z syndromes at i even and j odd. At step t every X syndrome
at position r swaps with the data qubit at r + v_t and every Z syndrome with
the data qubit at r + w_t, positions taken mod (l, m); a syndrome's check is
the set of data qubits it swapped with over the T steps, so the check weight
is T. For this code l = 40, m = 10, T = 11,
v = (1,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,0), (6,1), (6,1), (6,1), (1,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,0),
and w is the time reversal of v, as the paper's Table 4 states. The two check
families commute, k = 16 by GF(2) rank, every check has weight 11, and the
translation (i, j) -> (i + 1, j + 1) maps the X checks onto the Z checks, so
d_X = d_Z and a witness on one side maps to the other.

each to its stated (n, k) with its stated d as the reading:

## Evidence trail

Both witnesses were re-verified in GF(2) (zero syndrome against the opposite
checks, outside the row space of the same-type checks) before they were
written; verify/qldpc_verify.py re-checks them.

| instrument | trials | seed | lightest logical |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| accelerated RIS, pair depth 32 (research/kit/surrogate.py) | 200,000 | 5 | 17 |
| accelerated RIS, pair depth 32 | 2,000,000 | 5 | 17 |
| accelerated RIS, pair depth 32 | 10,000,000 | 41 | 17 (X logical) |
| the gate's own refutation (verify/validate_candidate.py) | 8,000 | gate | nothing below 17 |

The Z witness is the image of the X witness under the translation
(i, j) -> (i + 1, j + 1), which maps H_X onto H_Z, so the X-side budget covers
both sides. Final claim: d <= 17 on both sides, a witnessed upper bound at
10,000,000 trials per side; the paper states d = 17. The board's cell leaders
have been attacked at 300,000,000 GPU trials per side; this entry has not.

## Dead ends

Nine of the paper's ten instances do not advance the board: three are
already on it at the same parameters and six are dominated at check weight
7 or 11 by existing entries (table above). [[200,24,14]] holds its claimed
distance to 10,000,000 trials and is still out, because [[170,32,14]] has
fewer qubits and more logicals at the same distance and weight 10.

## Tools

Claude Fable 5.1 (Claude Code) with the repository kit: the routing rule
implemented in Python with NumPy, verify/gf2.py for ranks and witness
checks, research/kit/surrogate.py (fast backend) for the readings,
verify/validate_candidate.py for the gate, and a nauty canonical-form check
of the Tanner graph against the board and the literature data (no isomorphic
code found). About 25 minutes of laptop time for the ten instances including
the two 10,000,000-trial passes.

## Reproduction

Build the torus and the routing rule as described above with l = 40, m = 10
and the sequence v (w its reversal); index the data qubits in row-major order
of (i, j) over the sites with i + j even; the X check of the syndrome that
starts at (i, j) is the set of data qubits it swaps with over the 11 steps,
likewise for Z. The JSON carries the resulting check matrices and both
witnesses, so the entry can be verified without rerunning the construction.
The builder is committed with the campaign record of issue #2863.
